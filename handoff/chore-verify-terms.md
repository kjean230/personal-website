- **§9 item 1 — Vercel Hobby non-commercial terms. Settled: this site qualifies.** `https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines` (page metadata `last_updated: 2026-07-29`) states it twice over. The rule: "**Hobby teams** are restricted to non-commercial personal use only. All commercial usage of the platform requires either a Pro or Enterprise plan." The definition: "Commercial usage is defined as any Deployment that is used for the purpose of financial gain of **anyone** involved in **any part of the production** of the project, including a paid employee or consultant writing the code." Five examples are enumerated — requesting or processing payment, advertising the sale of a product or service, receiving payment to create/update/host the site, affiliate linking as the site's primary purpose, and advertisements including AdSense — plus a note that **"Asking for Donations fall under commercial usage."** A portfolio with no checkout, no ads, no affiliate links and no donate button matches none of them. The page's own instruction for that gap is to ask: "If you are unsure whether or not your site would be defined as commercial usage, please contact the Vercel Support team." **This finding rests on the enumerated examples, and the residual reading is flagged rather than resolved** — it is a one-email question for the owner if they want it airtight. - **§9 item 3 — Spotify, Steam and IGDB access terms. Settled, and Spotify does not permit the shape brief §6 specifies.** This is the finding that changes plan work, so it is stated first and in full. `https://developer.spotify.com/terms` (Effective 2025-05-15): **"Except as otherwise set out in these Developer Terms, you may not store, aggregate or create compilations or databases of Spotify Content, other than as strictly necessary to operate your SDA."** And on caching: **"Do not locally cache any Spotify Content, except as strictly necessary to enhance the performance of your SDA and its functionality, and limited to the temporary caching of: (1) metadata and cover art; or (2) Conditional Downloads of sound recordings."** Brief §6 specifies workers that "normalize into Postgres" so that "accumulated history (listening trends, playtime over months)" can be shown. **That is a database of Spotify Content, it is an aggregation, and months of retained history is not temporary caching — three separate collisions with one clause.** `feat/ingest-spotify` cannot be built as written. Also read, for whoever builds it: registration issues "one or more unique I.D.s, client I.Ds, keys, passwords, security codes, or tokens"; the platform licence covers Non-Streaming applications "for private personal use"; and there is a deletion duty — "when a user disconnects their Spotify Service account … you agree to delete and no longer request or process any of that user's Spotify Personal Data."
- **Spotify rate limits, for completeness** (`https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/concepts/rate-limits`): "Spotify's API rate limit is calculated based on the number of calls that your app makes to Spotify in a rolling 30 second window"; exceeding it yields 429 with "a `Retry-After` header with a value in seconds"; and "The limit varies depending on whether your app is in development mode or extended quota mode," development mode being "the default mode for new apps." A once-daily cron is nowhere near any of it. **Rate limits are not the problem with Spotify; the storage clause is.**
- **Steam — permits the shape, and adds two duties that land on `/privacy` rather than on the worker.** `https://steamcommunity.com/dev/apiterms`, read in full; the page carries no effective date beyond a "(c) 1996-2010 Valve Corporation" notice and reserves the right to change without notice. The licence: "you may access the Steam Web API, implement the Steam Web API in your Application, and distribute Steam Data to end users for their personal use via your Application." **There is no storage-duration limit and no prohibition on accumulating history** — Steam is the one of the three whose terms match brief §6 without strain. Rate limit: **"You are limited to one hundred thousand (100,000) calls to the Steam Web API per day"** against a plan of roughly one. Key handling matches the plan already: "You agree to keep your Steam Web API key confidential, and not to share it with any third party."
- **The two Steam duties, because they are requirements and not advice, and they are `/privacy`'s not the worker's.** **"You will post a privacy policy regarding the use of nonpublic end user data (including such Steam Data), and you will treat the Steam Data consistent with that policy."** And: **"You will inform the end user about any Steam Data you will store, and you will store the Steam Data in a country (or countries) identified in your privacy policy."** Brief §2.3 already requires shipping `/privacy`; this makes two of its clauses mandatory rather than optional — **name the Steam data stored, and name the country the Supabase project runs in.** `/privacy` is currently a reserved route in `lib/routes/table.ts` with no page, and Phase 3 owns it. **Steam ingestion must not ship before `/privacy` does**, which is a new ordering constraint between `lane/ingestion` and Phase 3 that no document states today. Also: "You will only retrieve Steam Data about a Steam end user as requested by the end user" — satisfied, since the owner is the end user and the subject.
- **One Steam clause that touches brief §2.1 and is therefore the owner's, not an agent's.** "You agree, and Valve grants you a license, to implement the Valve name(s), logo(s), and links to Valve (the 'Valve Brand & Links') on any Web page incorporating the Steam Web API and/or Steam Data … You shall not tag links to Valve hereunder with a 'nofollow' attribute or otherwise prevent or discourage search engines from following or scoring the link." Read as an obligation, that is a third-party wordmark on the Play Activity page, plus a `rel`-constrained outbound link. It is expressly *licensed* by the party that owns it, so it is not the unlicensed-asset problem brief §2.1 forbids — but §2.1 says assets are governed "without exception," so **the call is the owner's and is recorded as an open question rather than assumed.** The neighbouring restriction bounds it: Steam Data may not be presented so it appears "that your Application is endorsed or affiliated with Valve or Steam."
- **IGDB — access, cost and limits settled; storage blessed by IGDB and forbidden by the agreement IGDB names.** From `https://api-docs.igdb.com/` (reachable only with a browser user-agent; see the access note below): **"The IGDB.com API is free for non-commercial usage under the terms of the Twitch Developer Service Agreement."** Registration is a Twitch developer application yielding a Client ID and Client Secret, authenticating "as a Twitch Developer." Rate limit: **"There is a rate limit of 4 requests per second. If you go over this limit you will receive a response with status code 429 Too Many Requests. You are able to have up to 8 open requests at any moment in time."** Its FAQ answers the storage question directly and favourably: **"Am I allowed to store/cache the data locally? Yes. In fact, we prefer if you store and serve the data to your end users. You remain in control over your user experience, while alleviating pressure on the API itself."** Attribution is scoped to the paid tier — "as part of the partnership, we ask for user facing attribution to IGDB.com from products integrating the IGDB API" — so **a non-commercial integration is not required to attribute**, though doing so is free and courteous.
- **§9 item 4 — cover-art usage rights. Settled: do not display box art. The recommendation is to drop it, and the reason is that no source grants the right.** Three independent findings, none of which is a judgment call:
- **(a) The IGDB API documentation contains no rights language whatsoever.** Measured on the fetched page, not assumed: zero occurrences of "terms of service," "terms of use," "copyright" or "licence"; the 44 "commercial" hits are the pricing FAQ and the 23 "attribution" hits are an API field name plus that same FAQ. The documentation is purely technical. **There is no image-rights grant to find there.**
- **(b) The agreement IGDB names as governing says the opposite of IGDB's FAQ.** The Twitch Developer Services Agreement (`https://legal.twitch.com/en/legal/developer-agreement/`, "Last modified on 12/04/2024"): **"Do not store copies of Twitch Content or Program Materials, unless you: (a) obtain prior written authorization from Twitch (through these terms or otherwise); (b) control the rights associated with such content; or (c) cache such information for only a twenty-four hour time period without further sharing it with third parties. Re-syndication and re-distribution of Program Materials or data as available from a Twitch API is prohibited."** A twenty-four-hour cache with no onward sharing is the exact opposite of "we prefer if you store and serve the data to your end users." **IGDB's FAQ may well be the "(a) … prior written authorization … (through these terms or otherwise)" that the clause carves out — but that is an inference, and an inference is precisely what this item was opened to avoid.** Recorded as a documented conflict between two live sources, unresolved.
- **(c) Neither document says IGDB or Twitch may sublicense a publisher's cover art.** This is the decisive point and it is separate from (b). Cover art is the game publisher's copyright. The Twitch agreement states "The Program Materials are the intellectual property of Twitch or its licensors" and requires that you "not remove, modify, or obscure any copyright" notice — it nowhere grants downstream display rights in third-party artwork. **A permission to store data is not a licence to publish someone else's artwork.**
